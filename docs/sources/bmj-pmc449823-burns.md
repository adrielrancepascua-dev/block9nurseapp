Prose that is on the page:

- The palmar surface, including fingers, is roughly 0.8% of total body surface area. The page says this estimate is for relatively small burns (<15% of total surface area) or very large burns (>85%, counting unburnt skin).
- Wallace rule of nines is described as a quick estimate for medium to large burns in adults. “The body is divided into areas of 9%.” The page says it is not accurate in children.
- Lund and Browder is described as the most accurate method when used correctly, because it compensates for the variation in body shape with age. Age-by-region percentages: NOT FOUND in the text.
- Head 9, each arm 9, anterior trunk 18, posterior trunk 18, each leg 18, perineum 1: NOT FOUND in the text.
- Formal resuscitation is described for burns covering more than 15% of total body surface area in adults and more than 10% in children.
- Parkland formula table: total fluid in 24 hours = 4 ml × (total burn surface area (%)) × (body weight (kg)); 50% in the first 8 hours; 50% in the next 16 hours. Urine output end point 0.5–1.0 ml/kg/hour in adults and 1.0–1.5 ml/kg/hour in children.

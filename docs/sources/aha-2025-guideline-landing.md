Table 4. Categories of Blood Pressure in Adults:

```
BP Category     SBP                 DBP
Normal          <120 mm Hg     and  <80 mm Hg
Elevated        120 to 129 mm Hg and <80 mm Hg
Hypertension
Stage 1         130 to 139 mm Hg or  80 to 89 mm Hg
Stage 2         ≥140 mm Hg     or   ≥90 mm Hg
```

Footnote: adults with SBP and DBP in 2 categories should be designated to the higher BP category. The table excludes individuals who are pregnant (see Section 11.5). BP is an average of ≥2 careful readings on ≥2 occasions. The table is adapted from Whelton et al.

These four category cuts are the same as the 2017 summary table in `aha-2017-top-things.md`.

Severe hypertension and emergencies, section 6.2, copied:

“Hypertensive emergencies are defined as severe elevations in BP (>180/120 mm Hg) associated with evidence of acute target organ damage.”

The same section says severe hypertension without acute target organ damage should not have aggressive short-term lowering or parenteral therapy.

A low-BP category of systolic <90 mm Hg or diastolic <60 mm Hg: NOT FOUND in this text.

Pregnancy, quoting ACOG as printed in section 5.5:

Hypertension in pregnancy is SBP ≥140 mm Hg or DBP ≥90 mm Hg on 2 occasions at least 4 hours apart. Severe-range hypertension is sustained SBP ≥160 mm Hg or DBP ≥110 mm Hg, with verification in 15 minutes.

Table 24. Diagnostic criteria for preeclampsia, blood-pressure row:

“SBP ≥140 mm Hg or DBP ≥90 mm Hg on 2 occasions at least 4 h apart after 20 wks of gestation in a woman with previously normal BP or SBP ≥160 mm Hg or DBP ≥110 mm Hg.”

The table also requires proteinuria, or new-onset hypertension with thrombocytopenia, renal insufficiency, impaired liver function, pulmonary edema, or new-onset headache or visual symptoms. BP alone is not the whole criterion.
